ECON 238 · WEEK 11 · WRITTEN RESPONSES

Developing Another Little Ice Age

View Week 11 Poster (PDF) →

IDEA

Engineer an artificial volcano/mountain that can release reflective particles into the air to lower global temperatures. In the past it has been shown that volcanic eruptions have led to a decrease in the Earth’s temperature. The little ice age itself was primarily caused by multiple volcanic eruptions releasing large amounts of sulfur dioxide into the atmosphere over the time period. Sulfur dioxide reacts with particles in the air and converts to sulfates in the atmosphere which are reflective particles. Sulfur dioxide and sulfates do have harmful effects on the environment and humans. Instead, I propose to use an alternative reflective substance that can be dispersed into the atmosphere to reflect sunlight back out. This would increase the Earth’s albedo, which is what volcanic eruptions have done in the past. The manufactured volcano would then cover the Earth’s atmosphere with these particles for a duration of time. There would also be a resulting positive feedback loop where a reduction in temperature leads to greater ice coverage, raising Earth’s albedo and keeping Earth cooler.

1

The little ice age lowered global temperatures by 1℃, which would be a drastic improvement from where we currently are at roughly 1.4-1.5℃. To see a 1℃ decrease that would require the Earth’s to reflect 0.35% more sunlight, or absorb 1.2 watts/meter2 less solar energy. This would theoretically increase Earth’s albedo by 1.17%.

2

If this idea worked as intended, the benefits of lowering global temperatures would be widespread. The damage from global warming, rising sea levels, diseases such as malaria wouldn't be as prominent. Negative externalities from global warming would be reduced. Severe weather causes negative economic, agricultural, and infrastructural changes. Lowering the global temperature would hopefully solve a lot of these issues. However, negative externalities from fossil fuels wouldn't necessarily be solved. Air pollution for example would have to be solved from a reduction in emissions.

3

Currently a reduction in fossil fuels and greenhouse gas emissions are how countries are looking to reduce global temperatures. The benefit of doing things this way is not adding something to the mix, instead just reducing something that we are currently doing. This is less risky than adding particles into the atmosphere as we don't know the exact outcome. However, there is so much infrastructure using fossil fuels and the world relies on fossil fuels for almost everything. So there is an extent to how much reduction that could be feasibly done at this moment.

4

I think if the volcano idea came to fruition, oil companies wouldn’t push back against this. If this idea successfully was able to lower global temperatures, I could see oil companies not changing their output or even increasing their output as they no longer have to care about the negative externalities as much since we have a new policy to lower temperature. This idea has the potential to create a moral hazard where oil companies aren't feeling the pressure to lower their emissions as much any more.

5

I would say it is ok to tear down a “Chesterton Fence” when the only plausible solution to a problem requires removing the fence. Without fully understanding why the fence is there, I would say look at other options first, and then only if necessary remove the fence. For my idea, the “Chesterton Fence” is the atmosphere. Messing with the atmosphere and diffusing particles into it is a great risk as you can't determine all the unintended consequences that may occur. The lifetime of these particles is unknown, so is the removal strategy once the global temperature is at an acceptable level. My idea would be acceptable to tear down the fence if it's deemed the only practical solution to global warming. If lowering emissions or other policy changes aren't doing what they set out to do or in a fast enough time frame, then it would be ok to alter the atmosphere.

6

Farmers would be impacted due to a reduction in sunlight that can hurt crops and yields. This could impact farmers' incentive to stay in the farming business, or would incentivize them to switch farming practices. Switching to growing crops that don't require as much sunlight could become popular.

7

Governments may become less incentivized to uphold regulating oil companies or factories with high emissions, hurting business. It also depends on who is paying for the manufactured volcano. The government who bears the cost might still regulate companies to ensure global temperatures remain low and that the money they put into the volcano isn't wasted. However, other countries' governments may become lax with their regulation as they don't bear any of the costs or responsibility. The general public could also feel less responsible for lowering their carbon footprint.

8

The most obvious would probably be how reduced sunlight affects farming and the consequences of that. Fear of lower crop yields and nutrition density of the crops that do grow. Greenhouse or hydroponic farming are possible solutions to this issue. The 2nd obvious concern would be the uncertainty of the particles lifetime in the atmosphere/removal strategy of them. Would they affect the water supply when they come back down?
