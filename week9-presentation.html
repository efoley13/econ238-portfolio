Good Intentions, Unexpected Consequences

The economics of environmental justice and Indigenous land rights

WEEK 9 · 02 / 11

When a neighborhood gets cleaner, who wins?

Environmental improvement → rising housing demand → higher rents and property values. Homeowners may benefit, while renters may face displacement. **Question:** If original residents leave, was environmental justice achieved?

WEEK 9 · 03 / 11

The hidden price of quiet

Traffic noise is an environmental externality. Estimated capitalized nationwide burden: **$110 billion**. Illustrative annual equivalent at a 5% perpetual discount rate: **$5.5 billion/year**. Capitalized is not annual.

WEEK 9 · 04 / 11

Protection or investment barrier?

Massachusetts environmental justice review can provide more scrutiny of polluting projects. But added review costs and delays could also make some developers consider other sites. Does environmental quality improve? Does investment change?

WEEK 9 · 05 / 11

How would we know?

Difference-in-Differences: compare changes in EJ areas with changes in comparable non-EJ areas before and after a policy. Track projects, air quality and noise; check that trends were similar before the law.

WEEK 9 · 06 / 11

Property rights: the history behind the map

**1887 Dawes Act:** individual allotments and land loss. **1934 Indian Reorganization Act:** general allotment ends. **2009–2012 Cobell settlement:** compensation for trust-account mismanagement and funding for land consolidation.

WEEK 9 · 07 / 11

From ownership to opportunity

Energy and mineral development, recreation, and gaming all depend on property rights and regulatory processes. The **1988 Indian Gaming Regulatory Act** established a framework for gaming on eligible Indian lands. Legal permission does not always mean practical capacity.

WEEK 9 · 08 / 11

Land acknowledgments: words or action?

Our group discussed whether acknowledging dispossession without changing land use or ownership is enough. Beyond words: partnerships, shared stewardship, educational support, or financial commitments. What would meaningful accountability look like?

WEEK 9 · 09 / 11

Who bears the cost of speaking up?

Institutions may gain reputational benefits from official statements, while individuals may perceive social or professional costs from disagreeing. If silence is cheaper than dissent, does public agreement reveal private beliefs?

WEEK 9 · 10 / 11

Three questions worth taking with you

**01** Who benefits when a place improves? **02** Can protection unintentionally discourage opportunity? **03** When does acknowledgment become accountability?
